Four basic operators

An operator here is a matrix acting on state vectors. The one-qubit Pauli matrices are

I=(1001),X=(0110),I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
Y=(0−ii0),Z=(100−1).Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Their actions on basis states determine their actions on every superposition:

Operator Action on |0⟩\lvert0\rangle Action on |1⟩\lvert1\rangle
II |0⟩\lvert0\rangle |1⟩\lvert1\rangle
XX |1⟩\lvert1\rangle |0⟩\lvert0\rangle
YY i|1⟩i\lvert1\rangle −i|0⟩-i\lvert0\rangle
ZZ |0⟩\lvert0\rangle −|1⟩-\lvert1\rangle

Each matrix is unitary, Hermitian (meaning P†=PP^\dagger=P), and satisfies P2=IP^2=I. A nonzero vector satisfying P|v⟩=λ|v⟩P|v\rangle=\lambda|v\rangle is an eigenvector with eigenvalue λ\lambda. Applying PP twice gives |v⟩=λ2|v⟩|v\rangle=\lambda^2|v\rangle, so λ\lambda must be +1+1 or −1-1. The identity has only eigenvalue +1+1.

An nn-qubit Pauli string is a tensor product of nn such matrices. We usually omit ⊗\otimes: XZXZ means X⊗ZX\otimes Z, with the first letter acting on q0q_0. A signed string such as −XZ-XZ has one overall minus sign. For example, (−XZ)|01⟩=|11⟩(-XZ)|01\rangle=|11\rangle.

This differs from multiplying matrices on the same qubit: we write that product as X·ZX\cdot Z when needed. Tensor strings act on several qubits; matrix products compose operations. [1]

What does it mean to stabilize a state?

Definition (Stabilizer): A signed Pauli PP stabilizes |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle when

P|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩.P|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle.

This requires eigenvalue exactly +1+1. For example, ZZ stabilizes |0⟩|0\rangle, −Z-Z stabilizes |1⟩|1\rangle, and XX stabilizes |+⟩|+\rangle.

Why retain signs if global phase does not matter? The equation defines which eigenvalue the state has. Although Z|1⟩=−|1⟩Z|1\rangle=-|1\rangle represents the same physical state, it fails the required vector equality. Multiplying the input by any global phase cc still gives

Z(c|1⟩)=−c|1⟩.Z(c|1\rangle)=-c|1\rangle.

Thus its ZZ eigenvalue stays −1-1. Changing a state vector's global phase preserves its constraints; changing an operator's sign changes the constraint. Discarding signs would confuse |0⟩|0\rangle with |1⟩|1\rangle.

For the Bell state, both XXXX and ZZZZ are stabilizers. XXXX interchanges its two terms; ZZZZ multiplies |00⟩|00\rangle by 11 and |11⟩|11\rangle by (−1)(−1)=1(-1)(-1)=1. Therefore

XX|Φ+⟩=|Φ+⟩,XX|\Phi^+\rangle=|\Phi^+\rangle,
ZZ|Φ+⟩=|Φ+⟩.ZZ|\Phi^+\rangle=|\Phi^+\rangle.

These equations also explain the Bell state's amplitudes. The constraint ZZ|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩ZZ|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle forces the |01⟩|01\rangle and |10⟩|10\rangle amplitudes to zero, since ZZZZ negates those terms. The remaining constraint XX|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩XX|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle swaps |00⟩|00\rangle and |11⟩|11\rangle, forcing their amplitudes to agree. Normalization then gives |Φ+⟩|\Phi^+\rangle up to global phase.

Multiplication, commutation, and phases

Here is the complete single-qubit multiplication table. The row operator acts after the column operator: the row XX, column ZZ entry means X·Z=−iYX\cdot Z=-iY.

·\cdot II XX YY ZZ
II II XX YY ZZ
XX XX II iZiZ −iY-iY
YY YY −iZ-iZ II iXiX
ZZ ZZ iYiY −iX-iX II

Operators commute if PQ=QPPQ=QP: applying them in either order gives the same matrix. They anticommute if PQ=−QPPQ=-QP. The table shows that distinct nonidentity single-qubit Paulis anticommute; for example, X·Z=−iYX\cdot Z=-iY but Z·X=iYZ\cdot X=iY.

Multiply strings position by position, using (A⊗B)(C⊗D)=(AC)⊗(BD)(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=(AC)\otimes(BD). For example,

(XX)(ZZ)=(−iY)⊗(−iY)=−YY,(XX)(ZZ)=(-iY)\otimes(-iY)=-YY,
(ZZ)(XX)=(iY)⊗(iY)=−YY.(ZZ)(XX)=(iY)\otimes(iY)=-YY.

Thus XXXX and ZZZZ commute: reversing the order contributes one minus sign at each of two positions, and they cancel. In general, count the positions with distinct nonidentity letters. The strings commute if that count is even and anticommute if it is odd.

The Pauli group consists of all strings multiplied by +1,−1,+i,−i+1,-1,+i,-i; multiplication stays within this collection. Stabilizer constraints use its Hermitian elements, with overall phases ±1\pm1. Multiplying a string by ±i\pm i gives only imaginary eigenvalues, so it cannot fix a nonzero vector. [2]

Generators collect the constraints

If PP and QQ stabilize |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle, their product does too: PQ|ψ⟩=P|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩PQ|\psi\rangle=P|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle.

Definition (Stabilizer generators): A list of stabilizers g1,…,grg_1,\ldots,g_r generates all constraints obtained by multiplying its members. The resulting group is written ⟨g1,…,gr⟩\langle g_1,\ldots,g_r\rangle and includes the identity, obtained by taking no factors.

For commuting Hermitian generators, rearrange factors and cancel pairs using gj2=Ig_j^2=I. Each generator therefore need appear at most once. The Bell example gives

⟨XX,ZZ⟩={II,XX,ZZ,−YY}.\langle XX,ZZ\rangle=\{II,XX,ZZ,-YY\}.

We can store the two generators instead of the four group elements. Each stored row represents an equation about the whole state, not the state of an individual qubit.

Two anticommuting operators cannot both stabilize a nonzero vector: their products would give PQ|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩PQ|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle and PQ|ψ⟩=−QP|ψ⟩=−|ψ⟩PQ|\psi\rangle=-QP|\psi\rangle=-|\psi\rangle. Also, a consistent stabilizer group cannot contain −I-I, which would require −|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩-|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle.

For |0⟩⊗n|0\rangle^{\otimes n}, use Z0,…,Zn−1Z_0,\ldots,Z_{n-1}, where ZjZ_j applies ZZ to qjq_j and identities elsewhere. These constraints force every bit to 0. Next we prove when nn generators uniquely specify an nn-qubit state. [3]

Check your understanding

1. Keeping the sign

Does YYYY or −YY-YY stabilize |Φ+⟩|\Phi^+\rangle? Verify directly.

Show answer

Since Y|0⟩=i|1⟩Y|0\rangle=i|1\rangle and Y|1⟩=−i|0⟩Y|1\rangle=-i|0\rangle, we get YY|00⟩=−|11⟩YY|00\rangle=-|11\rangle and YY|11⟩=−|00⟩YY|11\rangle=-|00\rangle. Hence YY|Φ+⟩=−|Φ+⟩YY|\Phi^+\rangle=-|\Phi^+\rangle, so the stabilizer is −YY-YY.

2. Counting minus signs

Do XZXZ and ZXZX commute? What about XIXI and ZIZI?

Show answer

XZXZ and ZXZX differ in two nonidentity positions, so two minus signs cancel: they commute. XIXI and ZIZI differ in only one such position, so they anticommute. The identity letters contribute no minus sign.

3. Reading constraints

Which signs make ZIZI and IZIZ stabilize |10⟩|10\rangle?

Show answer

Use −ZI-ZI and +IZ+IZ. The first qubit is |1⟩|1\rangle, so ZIZI alone negates the vector; the extra minus sign cancels it. The second qubit is |0⟩|0\rangle, so IZIZ already fixes it. Their product, −ZZ-ZZ, is another stabilizer.
