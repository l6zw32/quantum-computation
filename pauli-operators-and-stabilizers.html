Instead of recording every amplitude of a state, we can record equations that the state satisfies. The equations we will use involve particularly simple matrices: the Pauli operators.

Four basic operators

The one-qubit Pauli matrices are

I=(1001),X=(0110),I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
Y=(0−ii0),Z=(100−1).Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Their actions on basis states determine their actions on every superposition:

Operator Action on |0⟩\lvert0\rangle Action on |1⟩\lvert1\rangle
II |0⟩\lvert0\rangle |1⟩\lvert1\rangle
XX |1⟩\lvert1\rangle |0⟩\lvert0\rangle
YY i|1⟩i\lvert1\rangle −i|0⟩-i\lvert0\rangle
ZZ |0⟩\lvert0\rangle −|1⟩-\lvert1\rangle

Each matrix is unitary, Hermitian (equal to its conjugate transpose), and squares to II. Consequently its eigenvalues can only be +1+1 or −1-1: a nonzero eigenvector satisfies P|v⟩=λ|v⟩P|v\rangle=\lambda|v\rangle, and applying PP again gives λ2=1\lambda^2=1. The identity has only eigenvalue +1+1.

An nn-qubit Pauli string is a tensor product of these matrices. We usually omit ⊗\otimes: XZXZ means X⊗ZX\otimes Z when describing a two-qubit string. Its first letter acts on q0q_0. To distinguish this from multiplying single-qubit matrices, we will write such a product as X·ZX\cdot Z when needed. A signed string such as −XZ-XZ carries one overall minus sign. [1]

What does it mean to stabilize a state?

We say a signed Pauli PP stabilizes |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle when

P|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩.P|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle.

This is a +1+1-eigenvector equation. For example, ZZ stabilizes |0⟩|0\rangle, −Z-Z stabilizes |1⟩|1\rangle, and XX stabilizes |+⟩|+\rangle.

The sign is part of the constraint. Although a common phase multiplying a state vector has no physical effect, Z|1⟩=−|1⟩Z|1\rangle=-|1\rangle does not make ZZ a stabilizer of |1⟩|1\rangle. The definition fixes the eigenvalue to +1+1. Multiplying |1⟩|1\rangle itself by any common phase leaves its ZZ eigenvalue equal to −1-1. Thus discarding generator signs would erase information distinguishing states.

For the Bell state, both XXXX and ZZZZ are stabilizers. XXXX interchanges its two terms; ZZZZ multiplies |00⟩|00\rangle by 11 and |11⟩|11\rangle by (−1)(−1)=1(-1)(-1)=1. Therefore

XX|Φ+⟩=|Φ+⟩,XX|\Phi^+\rangle=|\Phi^+\rangle,
ZZ|Φ+⟩=|Φ+⟩.ZZ|\Phi^+\rangle=|\Phi^+\rangle.

Multiplication, commutation, and phases

Here is the complete single-qubit multiplication table. The row operator acts after the column operator: the row XX, column ZZ entry means X·Z=−iYX\cdot Z=-iY.

·\cdot II XX YY ZZ
II II XX YY ZZ
XX XX II iZiZ −iY-iY
YY YY −iZ-iZ II iXiX
ZZ ZZ iYiY −iX-iX II

In particular, X·Y=iZX\cdot Y=iZ, Y·Z=iXY\cdot Z=iX, and Z·X=iYZ\cdot X=iY. Reversing any of these products changes its sign. Operators commute if PQ=QPPQ=QP and anticommute if PQ=−QPPQ=-QP. Distinct nonidentity single-qubit Paulis anticommute.

Multiply strings position by position, using (A⊗B)(C⊗D)=(AC)⊗(BD)(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=(AC)\otimes(BD). For example,

(XX)(ZZ)=(−iY)⊗(−iY)=−YY,(XX)(ZZ)=(-iY)\otimes(-iY)=-YY,
(ZZ)(XX)=(iY)⊗(iY)=−YY.(ZZ)(XX)=(iY)\otimes(iY)=-YY.

Thus XXXX and ZZZZ commute: the two local minus signs cancel. Generally, two strings commute exactly when the number of positions with distinct nonidentity letters is even.

The Pauli group consists of all strings multiplied by one of +1,−1,+i,−i+1,-1,+i,-i; these phases make multiplication closed. Stabilizer constraints use its Hermitian elements, whose overall phases are ±1\pm1. A string with phase ±i\pm i has eigenvalues ±i\pm i, so cannot fix a nonzero vector. [2]

Generators collect the constraints

If PP and QQ stabilize |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle, their product does too: PQ|ψ⟩=P|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩PQ|\psi\rangle=P|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle. The group generated by a list g1,…,grg_1,\ldots,g_r, written ⟨g1,…,gr⟩\langle g_1,\ldots,g_r\rangle, consists of all products of its members. For our commuting Hermitian Pauli generators, each generator need appear at most once because gj2=Ig_j^2=I. Our Bell example gives

⟨XX,ZZ⟩={II,XX,ZZ,−YY}.\langle XX,ZZ\rangle=\{II,XX,ZZ,-YY\}.

Two anticommuting operators cannot both stabilize a nonzero vector: their products would give PQ|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩PQ|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle and PQ|ψ⟩=−QP|ψ⟩=−|ψ⟩PQ|\psi\rangle=-QP|\psi\rangle=-|\psi\rangle. Also, a consistent stabilizer group cannot contain −I-I, which would require −|ψ⟩=|ψ⟩-|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle.

For the initial state |0⟩⊗n|0\rangle^{\otimes n}, use Z0,…,Zn−1Z_0,\ldots,Z_{n-1}, where ZjZ_j applies ZZ to qubit qjq_j and identities elsewhere. These commuting constraints say that every bit is 00. Next we will prove when a list of constraints specifies a unique state and how to store that list compactly. [3]

Check your understanding

Does YYYY or −YY-YY stabilize |Φ+⟩|\Phi^+\rangle? Verify directly.

Show answer

Since Y|0⟩=i|1⟩Y|0\rangle=i|1\rangle and Y|1⟩=−i|0⟩Y|1\rangle=-i|0\rangle, we get YY|00⟩=−|11⟩YY|00\rangle=-|11\rangle and YY|11⟩=−|00⟩YY|11\rangle=-|00\rangle. Hence YY|Φ+⟩=−|Φ+⟩YY|\Phi^+\rangle=-|\Phi^+\rangle, so the stabilizer is −YY-YY.
