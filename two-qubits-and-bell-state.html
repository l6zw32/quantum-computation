One qubit has amplitudes for two possible measurement outcomes. Two qubits have amplitudes for four joint outcomes. We will build a Bell state and show why its joint state cannot be described by assigning a separate pure state to each qubit.

Combining two qubits

We label the qubits q0q_0 and q1q_1, with q0q_0 always on the left. Thus |01⟩|01\rangle means that the first qubit is in |0⟩|0\rangle and the second is in |1⟩|1\rangle. The computational basis now has four vectors, in the order

|00⟩,|01⟩,|10⟩,|11⟩.|00\rangle,\quad |01\rangle,\quad |10\rangle,\quad |11\rangle.

These are the four standard coordinate vectors. A general pure state is

|ψ⟩=a|00⟩+b|01⟩+c|10⟩+d|11⟩,\begin{split} |\psi\rangle&=a|00\rangle+b|01\rangle\\ &\quad+c|10\rangle+d|11\rangle, \end{split}

where the complex amplitudes satisfy

|a|2+|b|2+|c|2+|d|2=1.|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2+|d|^2=1.

Throughout this section, measurement means computational-basis measurement. The Born rule works just as before:

Joint outcome Amplitude Probability
0000 aa |a|2|a|^2
0101 bb |b|2|b|^2
1010 cc |c|2|c|^2
1111 dd |d|2|d|^2

Measuring both qubits leaves the basis state observed. If we measure only q0q_0, find its outcome probabilities by grouping the outcomes with the same first bit:

Pr(q0=0)=|a|2+|b|2,Pr(q0=1)=|c|2+|d|2.\begin{split} \Pr(q_0=0)&=|a|^2+|b|^2,\\ \Pr(q_0=1)&=|c|^2+|d|^2. \end{split}

We add probabilities, not amplitudes: 0000 and 0101 are distinct basis outcomes. If measuring q0q_0 gives 0, the state retains the |00⟩|00\rangle and |01⟩|01\rangle terms, rescaled to be normalized. In particular, measuring one qubit need not fix the other qubit's value. [1]

Tensor products and product states

Suppose we independently prepare the first qubit in |u⟩=α|0⟩+β|1⟩|u\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle and the second in |v⟩=γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩|v\rangle=\gamma|0\rangle+\delta|1\rangle. How do their two-entry vectors become one four-entry vector?

Definition (Tensor product): The tensor product ⊗\otimes combines the vectors by multiplying every amplitude from the first by every amplitude from the second, in basis order:

(αβ)⊗(γδ)=(αγαδβγβδ).\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\\beta\end{pmatrix}\otimes \begin{pmatrix}\gamma\\\delta\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\gamma\\\alpha\delta\\\beta\gamma\\\beta\delta\end{pmatrix}.

In ket notation, use |x⟩⊗|y⟩=|xy⟩|x\rangle\otimes|y\rangle=|xy\rangle for bits x,yx,y, and expand distributively:

|u⟩⊗|v⟩=(α|0⟩+β|1⟩)⊗(γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩)=αγ|00⟩+αδ|01⟩+βγ|10⟩+βδ|11⟩.\begin{split} |u\rangle\otimes|v\rangle &=(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle)\otimes(\gamma|0\rangle+\delta|1\rangle)\\ &=\alpha\gamma|00\rangle+\alpha\delta|01\rangle\\ &\quad+\beta\gamma|10\rangle+\beta\delta|11\rangle. \end{split}

A state that can be written as |u⟩⊗|v⟩|u\rangle\otimes|v\rangle is called a product state. For example,

|+⟩⊗|0⟩=|00⟩+|10⟩2.|+\rangle\otimes|0\rangle =\frac{|00\rangle+|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}.

Its two nonzero amplitudes do not prevent it from being a product state. What matters is whether the whole vector factors.

Product states give independent measurement outcomes. For instance, Pr(11)=|βδ|2=|β|2|δ|2\Pr(11)=|\beta\delta|^2=|\beta|^2|\delta|^2. Summing all four probabilities gives (|α|2+|β|2)(|γ|2+|δ|2)=1(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)(|\gamma|^2+|\delta|^2)=1, so the joint vector is normalized when both input vectors are. [1]

Building the Bell state

For gates, A⊗BA\otimes B means apply AA to the first qubit and BB to the second. On product states,

(A⊗B)(|u⟩⊗|v⟩)=A|u⟩⊗B|v⟩.(A\otimes B)(|u\rangle\otimes|v\rangle) =A|u\rangle\otimes B|v\rangle.

Apply this rule to each term of a superposition using linearity. In particular, H⊗IH\otimes I applies H only to q0q_0; the identity gate II leaves q1q_1 unchanged.

The controlled-NOT gate (CNOT) has a control qubit and a target qubit. Here q0q_0 is the control and q1q_1 the target: it leaves the control unchanged and flips the target exactly when the control bit is 1.

Input basis state Output basis state
|00⟩|00\rangle |00⟩|00\rangle
|01⟩|01\rangle |01⟩|01\rangle
|10⟩|10\rangle |11⟩|11\rangle
|11⟩|11\rangle |10⟩|10\rangle

Equivalently, |xy⟩↦|x,y⊕x⟩|xy\rangle\mapsto|x,y\oplus x\rangle, where ⊕\oplus is addition modulo 2. This permutation of basis vectors is unitary. The table specifies CNOT on the four basis states, but CNOT can also take any superposition of them as input, applying these rules term by term by linearity. [2]

Example: prepare the Bell state

Start in |00⟩|00\rangle and apply these two gates, with no measurement between them.

Step 1: apply H to the first qubit. Since H|0⟩=(|0⟩+|1⟩)/2H|0\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2,

(H⊗I)|00⟩=(H|0⟩)⊗|0⟩=|00⟩+|10⟩2.\begin{split} (H\otimes I)|00\rangle &=(H|0\rangle)\otimes|0\rangle\\ &=\frac{|00\rangle+|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}. \end{split}

Step 2: apply CNOT. The |00⟩|00\rangle term stays fixed, while |10⟩|10\rangle becomes |11⟩|11\rangle:

CNOT|00⟩+|10⟩2=CNOT|00⟩+CNOT|10⟩2=|00⟩+|11⟩2.\begin{split} &\operatorname{CNOT}\frac{|00\rangle+|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}\\ &\quad=\frac{\operatorname{CNOT}|00\rangle+\operatorname{CNOT}|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}\\ &\quad=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}. \end{split}

The final state is the Bell state we will use throughout the guide, denoted

|Φ+⟩=|00⟩+|11⟩2.|\Phi^+\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}.

Measuring both qubits gives 0000 or 1111, each with probability 1/21/2. If we measure only q0q_0, we get 0 or 1 equally often. Conditioned on that outcome, the joint state becomes |00⟩|00\rangle or |11⟩|11\rangle, respectively, so a subsequent measurement of q1q_1 agrees with q0q_0.

Why the Bell state is entangled

Definition (Entanglement of a pure state): A pure state of two qubits is entangled if it cannot be written as a product of two one-qubit state vectors.

Suppose |Φ+⟩|\Phi^+\rangle were (α|0⟩+β|1⟩)⊗(γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩)(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle)\otimes(\gamma|0\rangle+\delta|1\rangle). The tensor-product expansion would require

αγ=1/2,αδ=0,βγ=0,βδ=1/2.\begin{split} \alpha\gamma&=1/\sqrt2,\qquad \alpha\delta=0,\\ \beta\gamma&=0,\qquad\quad \beta\delta=1/\sqrt2. \end{split}

Since αγ≠0\alpha\gamma\ne0, both α\alpha and γ\gamma are nonzero. Likewise, βδ≠0\beta\delta\ne0 makes both β\beta and δ\delta nonzero. But then αδ\alpha\delta cannot be 0: a contradiction. The Bell state is therefore entangled. Allowing a global phase does not change this conclusion, since that phase could be absorbed into one tensor factor.

Entanglement versus classical correlation

A fair coin could tell us to prepare |00⟩|00\rangle or |11⟩|11\rangle. That mixture has the same measurement probabilities as |Φ+⟩|\Phi^+\rangle, but the preparations differ. To see this, apply H to both qubits before measuring:

(H⊗H)|Φ+⟩=|+⟩⊗|+⟩+|−⟩⊗|−⟩2=|Φ+⟩.\begin{split} (H\otimes H)|\Phi^+\rangle &=\frac{|+\rangle\otimes|+\rangle+|-\rangle\otimes|-\rangle}{\sqrt2}\\ &=|\Phi^+\rangle. \end{split}

In the last equality, expanding the two products cancels the |01⟩|01\rangle and |10⟩|10\rangle terms. The Bell state still gives only 0000 and 1111.

The coin-selected preparation instead becomes a random choice between |+⟩⊗|+⟩|+\rangle\otimes|+\rangle and |−⟩⊗|−⟩|-\rangle\otimes|-\rangle. Each product gives every two-bit outcome with probability 1/41/4, so their mixture does too. Here we average probabilities across the coin's choices; we do not add amplitudes from separate preparations. This is why matching probabilities in one measurement basis does not establish that two states are the same.

From two qubits to many

For nn qubits, the same pattern gives

|ψ⟩=∑x∈{0,1}nαx|x⟩,∑x|αx|2=1.|\psi\rangle=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}\alpha_x|x\rangle, \qquad \sum_x|\alpha_x|^2=1.

There are 2n2^n basis strings, so explicitly listing all amplitudes uses 2n2^n entries. The next sections develop a much shorter description for a useful family of states, including this entangled Bell state.

Check your understanding

1. Expanding a product state

Expand |+⟩⊗|−⟩|+\rangle\otimes|-\rangle in the computational basis. What are the four measurement probabilities? Is it entangled?

Show answer

Distribute the two factors:

|+⟩⊗|−⟩=|00⟩−|01⟩+|10⟩−|11⟩2.|+\rangle\otimes|-\rangle =\frac{|00\rangle-|01\rangle+|10\rangle-|11\rangle}{2}.

Every amplitude has magnitude 1/21/2, so every outcome has probability 1/41/4. The state is a product state by construction, even though all four amplitudes are nonzero. It is not entangled.

2. Applying H to the other qubit

Start in |00⟩|00\rangle, apply H to q1q_1 rather than q0q_0, then apply CNOT with control q0q_0. Is the result entangled?

Show answer

The first gate produces (|00⟩+|01⟩)/2(|00\rangle+|01\rangle)/\sqrt2. CNOT fixes both terms because their control bit is 0. The result is |0⟩⊗|+⟩|0\rangle\otimes|+\rangle, a product state. CNOT can create entanglement, but does not do so for every input.

3. A different starting state

Start in |10⟩|10\rangle, apply H to q0q_0, then apply CNOT with control q0q_0. Find the final state and its measurement probabilities. Is it the same physical state as |Φ+⟩|\Phi^+\rangle?

Show answer

Since H|1⟩=(|0⟩−|1⟩)/2H|1\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2, the two steps give

|10⟩⟼|00⟩−|10⟩2⟼|00⟩−|11⟩2.\begin{split} |10\rangle&\longmapsto\frac{|00\rangle-|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}\\ &\longmapsto\frac{|00\rangle-|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}. \end{split}

The outcomes are still 0000 and 1111, each with probability 1/21/2. But the minus sign changes the relative phase between the two nonzero amplitudes. A global factor preserving the |00⟩|00\rangle amplitude must be 1, so it cannot change the sign of the |11⟩|11\rangle amplitude. This is a different physical state from |Φ+⟩|\Phi^+\rangle.

Carry forward: tensor products combine independent pure-state preparations; entangled states require a joint description. Gates act on amplitudes, and measurement probabilities alone can hide the relative phases that matter later.
